The cerebellum's main role is coordinating smooth, precise movement.
It does this by constantly comparing the motor commands your brain
sends to the sensory feedback coming back — proprioception from
muscles and joints, balance from the inner ear — and making
moment-to-moment corrections that keep movement on target. Think
about catching a ball: your hand arrives in the right place at the
right time without you consciously calculating any of it. In OT
practice, cerebellar damage produces ataxia — uncoordinated, jerky
movement that disrupts everyday activities like buttoning a shirt
or pouring a cup of coffee, which is why we care about this
structure's function.
